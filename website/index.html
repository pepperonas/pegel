Pegel is a system equalizer for Android: ten bands, bass, loudness and virtualizer — and a profile for every pair of headphones that loads by itself. No ads, no tracking, no account.

Everything an equalizer needs. Nothing else.

Ten bands, half-decibel steps

From 31 Hz to 16 kHz, ±12 dB each, with a curve above the sliders that shows what you are doing. 22 presets to start from, and your own next to them.

Bass, loudness, virtualizer

Each with one strength slider. Loudness pushes its gain into a limiter, so a boost gets denser instead of clipping. Reverb and balance are there when you want them.

Profiles that follow your headphones

Save the whole sound under a name and give each output its own: speaker, wired, USB, every Bluetooth device. Plug in or connect, and Pegel loads the right one.

Honest about what it reaches

Without root, an equalizer reaches the global mix and the players that announce their audio. Pegel uses both, and one line at the top always says what it is attached to right now.

Backup and sharing

All profiles, presets and settings in one JSON file — and back. A single profile travels as a .pegel file to anyone who has the app.

Nothing but sound

No ads, no analytics, no account, no paid version. The app goes online for one thing only — its own, checksum-verified updates — and even that can be switched off.

Install in three steps

  1. 1

    Download the APK

    One file for every phone with Android 9 or later — there is nothing to pick.

  2. 2

    Allow the install

    Open the file. Android asks once whether your browser may install apps — allow it.

  3. 3

    Switch Pegel on

    The switch is at the top right. If a player does not react, look for “system equalizer” in its audio settings — the in-app help lists what to try.

Questions

Is Pegel free?

Yes. It is free and open source under the MIT licence — no ads, no account, no tracking, and no paid version with more features.

Does it work with every app?

No equalizer without root does. Pegel reaches what plays through the phone's global mix and every player that announces its audio session; some players need “system equalizer” switched on in their settings. Apps with their own audio engine or copy protection may stay unaffected.

Does anything leave my phone?

No. Pegel does not record or analyse audio — it only configures effects that Android runs. The only network access is the update check: it reads the newest version number from this page, without any identifier. Switch it off in Settings and the app makes no request at all.

Which phones does it run on?

Android 9 or later — the audio engine Pegel builds on does not exist before that. On phones with aggressive battery savers, exempt the app from battery optimization; Settings offers it.

How do I update?

Inside the app. When a new version is out, a card appears on the first screen: Update downloads the file, checks its SHA-256 and hands it to Android to install. Your profiles and settings stay. You can also download the APK here and install it over the app.

Is the file genuine?

Compare the SHA-256 checksum and the signing certificate shown on this page. Android also refuses to install an update signed with a different key over the app.
